Understanding the Forecast

KEY TERMS USED IN THE ARIMA MODEL AND THIS DASHBOARD

NET Net Trade

The difference between a country's oil exports and imports. Positive means net exporter (exports > imports), negative means net importer (imports > exports). This shows whether a country is a supplier or consumer of oil on the global market.

Net = Exports − Imports
KBD Thousand Barrels per Day

The standard unit for measuring oil trade volume. 1 KBD = 1,000 barrels of oil flowing per day. This is the unit used across all values in this dashboard.

5,000 KBD = 5 million barrels/day
p Auto-Regressive Order

The number of past values (lags) the model uses to predict the next value. A higher p means the model looks further back in history.

p=2 → uses the last 2 years to predict
d Differencing Order

How many times the data is differenced to make it stationary (removing trends). A value of d=1 means we look at year-over-year changes instead of raw values.

d=1 → model uses changes, not levels
q Moving Average Order

The number of past forecast errors the model incorporates. This helps smooth out noise and capture shock effects.

q=1 → corrects using 1 past error
MAPE Mean Absolute Percentage Error

Measures forecast accuracy as a percentage. Lower is better. Under 5% is excellent, 5–10% is good, above 10% suggests volatility.

MAPE 3.2% → avg error of 3.2% per year
95% CI Confidence Interval

The shaded band around forecast lines. There's a 95% probability the true value falls within this range. Wider bands = more uncertainty.

Wider band in 2030 vs 2025 = more uncertainty
