Key insight

⚡ Why EV adoption matters

In countries with high oil import dependency, every percentage-point gain in EV market share translates directly to reduced crude imports — freeing foreign-exchange reserves that can be redirected toward electricity grid and infrastructure investment.

🛢️ Oil import dependency

Non-oil-producing nations spend a significant share of GDP on oil imports. This dashboard tracks whether that burden is easing as EV fleets grow.

About this project

Methods & Data Sources

This dashboard uses freely available IEA datasets to investigate one question: does growing EV adoption demonstrably reduce oil imports in non-oil-producing nations, and how large could the fiscal dividend be?

📊

Data Sources

Oil import/export figures are drawn from the IEA Oil Information database. EV sales and market-share data come from the IEA Global EV Outlook. Coverage: 50+ countries, 2000–2023.

🔬

Methodology

We compare country-level annual EV market share to year-over-year changes in oil import volumes, controlling for GDP growth. The investment-redirect estimate applies a conservative average oil price to the barrels displaced and scales to per-capita grid investment benchmarks.

⚠️

Caveats

Oil demand is influenced by many factors beyond EV adoption (industrial demand, heating, shipping). This analysis focuses on light-vehicle transport fuel only and should be read as indicative, not causal.
